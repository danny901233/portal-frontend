Tyre-replacement calls are short, price-sensitive, and full of traps. Quote the wrong size, miss a TPMS note, or fail to offer an in-stock swap and the caller rings the next place. This guide shows how to handle tyre enquiries on the phone with a simple price ladder, sensible brand swaps, and an AI flow that gets the job booked. ## What tyre callers actually want

Most callers are trying to answer three questions fast:
- Do you have the right tyre today?
- How much, fully fitted?
- When can you do it?

They rarely call with a clean brief. Expect gaps and mix-ups you must straighten out quickly:
- Size vs reg mismatch: “205/55 R16” on the last invoice but the car now runs 17s; or a facelift model with different fitment. Start with the reg, then confirm size from your system or with a clear read-back.
- Load/speed ratings and XL: many cars (and most SUVs/EVs) need reinforced (XL) tyres; vans need C-rated. Quote the correct spec, not just the size.
- Run-flat vs conventional: swapping changes ride and legality on some setups; be explicit.
- EV/foam-lined options: lower noise, torque-ready casings; good to offer, not always required.
- Puncture repair vs replace: if the damage is in the central 75% and within BSAU159 standards, repair may be viable; set expectations and offer both paths.
- Same-day pressure: callers want today or tomorrow. If not in stock, offer a clear next-best (brand swap, different depot, or AM delivery with PM fitting).

Avoid partial quotes. Give an out-the-door fitted price that includes:
- Tyre, valve, balancing, casing disposal, VAT.
- TPMS service kit if applicable (often £5–£12 per valve).
- Any surcharges (SUV/4x4, run-flat handling) if you use them.
- Optional add-ons (alignment check, puncture repair) as separate line items.

## A price ladder that works on the phone

A simple ladder converts better than a single number. Offer three options per axle that match the caller’s need and your stock position.

How to build it:
1. Map your common sizes (e.g. 205/55 R16, 225/45 R17, 195/65 R15, 225/40 R18, popular SUV and EV sizes). For each, define three tiers: Budget, Mid, Premium.
2. Set fitted, out-the-door prices per tyre for each tier, inclusive of valve, balance, disposal, VAT. Keep these current with supplier feeds and your margins.
3. Add rules for XL, run-flat, C-rated van, and EV/foam-lined variants (these are usually a separate ladder or a clear surcharge).
4. Decide alignment policy: quote a quick check included and full geometry from a separate price (commonly £30–£45 + VAT for a front toe set, more for full geometry). Don’t bury it.

Example (illustrative only; use your numbers and brands):
- Budget: from £55–£75 fitted per tyre on 205/55 R16
- Mid-range: from £80–£110 fitted
- Premium: from £115–£160 fitted
Notes:
- Add £8–£15 for XL/run-flat on many sizes.
- TPMS service kit: £5–£12 per valve if disturbed.
- Pair pricing: offer the total for two with a clear figure (“Two mid-range fitted today: £198 all-in”).

On the phone, present it like this:
- Confirm size/spec: “I’ve got 205/55 R16 91V for your Golf.”
- Availability: “In stock today.”
- Ladder, succinct: “Good at £72, better at £96, best at £132 per tyre fitted. The mid is quieter and lasts longer in daily use.”
- Close the gap: “Shall I book a pair at £192 all-in this afternoon? 2:15 or 3:40?”

If stock is thin, swap tiers intelligently. Never say “we don’t have it” without an alternative and a firm time.

## Brand swaps and tricky fitments, made simple

Callers often ask for a specific brand. Offer it if you can. If not, swap with logic and a reason:
- Same tier, same label story: match wet grip/noise ratings and country of manufacture where relevant.
- Trust anchors: “This mid-range option is OE on several models in your segment” beats vague claims.
- Explain the difference in plain English: “Premium gives shorter wet braking and tends to last longer on motorway miles.”

Edge cases your phone flow should catch and handle:
- Run-flat only on cars designed for it (many BMW/MINI): be clear if changing design.
- Staggered fitments (wider rears): quote pairs correctly and check directional/asymmetric patterns.
- Vans and pickups: C-rated (reinforced) tyres, not car tyres; check load index carefully.
- EV-marked options: lower noise and stronger casing; offer as an upgrade, not a must.
- XL requirement: many crossovers need XL; don’t downgrade.
- Winter vs all-season: if the caller mentions grip in cold/snow or lives rurally, offer all-season as an option with a price and lead time.
- TPMS: if sensors are seized or corroded, sleeves and seals may be needed; set a “from” price and note we’ll confirm on inspection.
- Locking wheel nut: ask the caller to bring it; note it on the booking.
- Repair vs replacement: screen for puncture position and run-flat status; quote repair price and time (“while you wait”) alongside replacement.

Keep the advice consistent: don’t mix patterns on the same axle; if only one tyre is worn, explain why a pair is safer and often required by policy.

## The AI flow that turns the call into a booked job

A tight, predictable script converts tyre calls and avoids ping-pong. This is how ReceptionMate runs it on the phone:

1. Greet and intent: “Are you after a puncture repair or replacement today?”
2. Vehicle capture: take the reg, confirm make/model/derivative; read back size/spec from your system; if the caller reads the sidewall, confirm format (width/profile rim, load/speed, XL/run-flat).
3. Use case and budget: “Do you have a brand in mind, or shall I give you good/better/best?”
4. Stock and cut-offs: check live stock and supplier feeds; if not same-day, offer the next courier window (“AM delivery, PM fitting”). Mention typical cut-offs without promising the impossible.
5. Present the ladder: three clear fitted prices, with a one-sentence benefit per tier. Quote totals for a pair or full set as relevant.
6. Handle swaps and questions: brand alternatives, EV/run-flat/XL clarifications, noise/wet grip labels, warranty. Keep it short and confident.
7. Book the slot: propose two concrete times. Block an appropriate duration (e.g. 30 mins per tyre; add time for run-flats/TPMS).
8. Capture essentials: name, mobile, email, locking wheel nut reminder, any notes (staggered sizes, mobile fitting address if offered).
9. Deposits and links: if you take deposits for special orders, send a secure payment link during the call and hold the slot on payment.
10. Confirm and push: send SMS/email confirmation with price breakdown and what’s included; push the job into the diary and, if supported, reserve stock.

Escalation rules matter. If the caller is unhappy with all swaps, wants a niche brand, or the system flags unusual sizing, route to a human with the transcript and a prebuilt quote so they don’t start from scratch.

Practical setup that makes this work:
- Preload price ladders for your top 20 sizes, plus rules for XL, run-flat, C-rated, EV.
- Define brand preference lists per tier (what to offer first, second, third).
- Set policies: pair-only on driven axles, alignment check defaults, TPMS pricing, deposits.
- Enter supplier cut-off times and lead-time promises you’re happy to keep.
- Keep pricing aligned with your website and walk-in boards to avoid awkward conversations.

Handled this way, tyre calls become predictable revenue, not a race to the bottom. Clear ladders, sensible swaps, and a booking-first mindset win more often than “let me call you back.”
